Blast Radius: What File Locks for Coding Agents Actually Prevent

Joshua Pinckard
University of California Riverside
2026

# Abstract

Coordination layers for concurrent coding agents are being built on a quantity
nobody has measured: how often concurrent edits actually conflict, and what
shape the conflict takes when they do. The systems published in 2026 claim
regions at file granularity and refuse conflicting work fail-closed, treating
same-file concurrency as unsafe by default. We measured the assumption by
deterministically replaying every two-parent merge in 16 repositories across 6
languages (25,073 merges), finding 416 conflicted (1.66%) with per-repository
rates spanning 0% to 56.6%. Conflict probability rises monotonically with
divergence from the merge base in 12 of 12 informative repositories (AUC 0.815,
repository bootstrap 0.735 to 0.885), climbing from 0 of 5,356 merges at one
commit of combined divergence to 195 of 2,100 (9.3%) at 128 commits or more.
Within handwritten conflicted files the median conflicted span is 10.7% of the
file's bytes and whole-file conflicts are 23 of 845 (2.7%), while the two
sides' changed bytes genuinely intersect in 304 of 372 decidable conflicted
merges (81.7%). A file-granularity claim would have serialized 475 of 684
same-file concurrent path units (69.4%) that merged cleanly, and even inside
globally conflicted merges 249 of 458 same-file units (54.4%) did not conflict.
Lockfiles are the exception with a median conflicted span of 100% of the file,
showing the correct claim granularity is stratified by file type rather than
uniform. The over-blocking figure is one repository's full anchored census
(Click) and the anatomy figures are corpus-wide.

The measurements are validated in four ways. First a semantic replay of the 44
most recent mechanically clean Click merges with both parents passing tests
found 0 of 44 broke the suite (13 of the 44 contained same-file concurrency
and 4 contained concurrent source paths, the informative subset), and the
recorded merge tree differed from the mechanical merge in 1 of 398 clean
merges. Second an audit of 35 production
reports from a live multi-agent codebase found only 9 of 13 contested paths
fully attributable (69.2%) when ownership rests on agent self-report, and 0 of
11 real collisions were decided by textual mergeability alone. Third a causal
probe that applies a historical change, perturbs another file, and reruns the
change's own tests measured true dependency at 9.5% to 29% of swept files per
change, established by intervention rather than by the field's proxy-vs-proxy
convention. Fourth a frozen corpus of 6,290 agent transcripts showed subagent
file operations carry usable claim metadata in 0 of 48,303 events, showing
mechanical capture at the mediation layer is the only channel that can exist.
The remaining axis is agent-conditioned contention. Re-enacted conflict sites
with real agents under five coordination arms will measure it, with 107 mined
candidate sites carrying tests on both sides.

# 1. Introduction

## Background Information

Coding agents now edit shared repositories concurrently and the systems that
coordinate them are converging on a single design. An agent declares the files
it intends to touch, a coordinator grants or refuses the claim, and refused
work waits. The declared unit is the file and the refusal is fail-closed.
Concurrent human development solved the same problem differently, abandoning
lock-modify-unlock for copy-modify-merge in the 1990s and resolving concurrent
edits at integration with a line-granular three-way merge. The agent systems
are reintroducing the locking model without the measurement that would justify
its granularity, its refusal posture, or its cost.

## Research Problem

How often concurrent work on the same file actually conflicts is unknown, and
so is the byte-level shape of real conflicts when they occur. It is also
unknown whether a textually clean merge can be trusted semantically, how
conflict probability grows as two lines of work diverge, and whether
agent-declared claims can be attributed after the fact. Change-impact research
compounds the gap by validating co-change predictors against other co-change
predictors, leaving no ground truth established by intervention. These numbers
decide real designs. A coordinator that over-blocks wastes the concurrency it
exists to enable and a coordinator that under-blocks corrupts work silently.

# 2. Literature Review

Optimistic concurrency control (Kung and Robinson, 1981) validates a
transaction's read set at write time and derives that read set mechanically
during execution, never by declaration. The 2026 agent coordination systems
(Claim Plane, ATM, CoAgent) invert this, accepting declared file claims and
refusing overlap fail-closed, and STORM names file-granularity false rejection
as an unquantified failure mode of its own design. Speculative merging (Brun
et al., 2011) merged concurrent developer branches ahead of time and found
textual conflicts plus test-breaking clean merges, establishing the method our
semantic replay reuses. Co-change mining (Zimmermann et al., 2004) predicts
related files from version history, and Rolfsnes and Moonen (2016) compared 39
association measures with confidence and support already in the top class.
None of these works measures the byte-level anatomy of real conflicts, none
derives claims mechanically for agents, and none validates change impact
against ground truth produced by intervention.

Patch theory gives the measurement an algebraic identity. In the categorical
accounts of version control (Mimram and Di Giusto, 2013, the Darcs patch
formalism, and homotopical patch theory, Angiuli et al., 2014) a merge is a
pushout and disjoint patches are exactly the pairs that commute. Under that
reading this study measured the empirical density of commutation in real
development, with 69.4% of same-file concurrent units commuting and 81.7% of
conflicts being genuine failures to commute. A mediator that validates byte
regions at write time is enforcing conflict serializability at byte
granularity, connecting the design to Kung and Robinson (1981) rather than
to locking.

## Research Questions

- What fraction of real two-parent merges conflict, and how does the rate vary
  by repository, language, and workflow?
- How does conflict probability change with divergence from the merge base
  measured in commits, wall-clock time, and changed lines?
- What fraction of same-file concurrent edits would a file-granularity claim
  serialize needlessly, and what fraction of real conflicts genuinely
  intersect at byte level?
- What fraction of textually clean merges break the test suite when both
  parents pass it?
- What fraction of files does a change causally depend on, established by
  perturbing other files and rerunning the change's own tests?
- Can ownership of contested writes be attributed when claims are
  agent-declared, and can it be attributed mechanically?
- Do agents under different coordination designs reproduce the contention that
  human history records, or sit elsewhere on the same divergence curve?

## Hypothesis

We hypothesize that file-granularity claiming over-blocks the majority of
same-file concurrent work (rate above 0.5) and that real conflicts intersect
at byte level in the majority of cases, leaving a small disjoint minority for
a test gate to carry. We hypothesize the silent breakage rate of clean merges
is below 0.10 per repository with at least one breakage observable somewhere,
and that conflict probability rises monotonically with divergence. We further
hypothesize that human history understates agent contention because the human
base rate is produced by social coordination and early integration, while the
divergence curve itself transfers. The curve is close to mechanical (more work
in flight on both sides raises the chance of intersection) and the operating
point on it is what a scheduler chooses.

# 3. Methodology

## Research Design

The study is observational replay plus intervention. Four instruments produce
the measurements: a deterministic merge-conflict miner over real history, a
semantic replay that executes tests on mechanically merged trees, a causal
dependency probe that perturbs files against a pinned answer key, and a
production corpus audit of agent-written collision reports. A fifth instrument
(the arms ladder) is designed and gated but not yet run, and is reported in
future tense throughout. The analysis workers are themselves agents, and this
paper finds agent self-report only 69.2% attributable, so no analysis result
rests on worker testimony. Every instrument's output is checked by mechanism
instead, byte-identical double runs for the miner, pinned environments and
raw pytest artifacts for the replay and probe, and per-file hashes with
quoted line evidence for the corpus audit.

## Conflict Mining

Every exactly-two-parent merge reachable by first parent is replayed with git
merge-tree against task-owned bare mirrors, recording conflict status, paths,
and conflicted byte ranges without touching a working tree. The environment is
fully pinned (scrubbed GIT_* variables, null global config, fixed merge and
diff settings) and the interpretation rule is fixed in advance, exit 0 clean
and exit 1 conflicted and anything else an operational failure. 16
repositories were chosen for language and shape coverage rather than
popularity, spanning Python, Go, Rust, Java, C/C++, and JavaScript across
libraries, applications, and infrastructure. The full miner was run twice on 4
repositories and produced byte-identical outputs, and 10 of 25,083 merges were
unevaluable. Conflicted files are classified handwritten, generated, lockfile,
or vendored by a stated path rule and never pooled silently.

## Semantic Replay

For clean merges the mechanical merge tree is materialized in a fresh worktree
and the repository's own suite is the answer key. A merge enters the
denominator only when both parents pass the suite independently, and a red
merged tree is rerun once with disagreement excluded as flaky. The replay
covered the 44 most recent mechanically clean divergent Click merges under a
frozen budget rule (pilot timing, outcome-blind cap, newest first), with the
354 older clean merges recorded as beyond cap rather than excluded. The
recorded merge commit's tree is also compared to the mechanical tree for all
398 clean merges, any difference marking developer intervention after a clean
merge. Pygments was intended as the second repository and its fixture failed
its materialization gate, which is recorded as a result rather than repaired.

## Causal Dependency Probe

A historical change is applied so its focal tests pass, another file is
perturbed by inserting a raise statement at the top of every function body,
and the focal tests are rerun. A red result means the change causally depended
on the perturbed file and a green result means it did not. Every task is
verified green-red-green in advance (green on the base with tests absent, red
with the test patch applied without the source patch, green with both),
making each task a probe rather than a corpus entry. The probe measures
test-observable runtime dependency rather than causal dependency in general,
a function the focal tests never exercise comes back green even when it is
semantically coupled, so every rate it produces is a lower bound and is named
as one. The fixture pins pallets/click at a five-run deterministic suite
gate, and 13 initial tasks plus 40 expansion tasks are validated with one
task's non-reproduction between runs (9 of 31 vs 10 of 31) carried as an open
instrument caveat.

## Production Corpus

35 reports written by concurrent agents working one live codebase were frozen
with per-file hashes and mined for collisions, defined as two or more tasks
targeting the same file, symbol, or hunk. Each collision row records the
colliding identities, the winner, the stated basis, and whether every side was
attributable, with three alternative counting rules reported as sensitivity.
The reports are agent testimony rather than instrumentation and every derived
claim carries that limitation. Where a report's claim about the code was
checkable against the frozen code it was checked, finding 11 contradictions
between reports or between a report and the code.

## Transcript Corpus

6,290 agent-session transcripts (4.15 GB) were frozen by byte prefix under a
recorded SHA-256 and scraped for operational parameters, event rates, read
and edit region sizes, read multiplicity per file, concurrency per minute,
and capture coverage by channel. Shell commands outnumber structured file
calls 3.29 to 1 and a stratified 50-command audit of the best lexical path
parser measured 35.2% recall, so shell mentions are never treated as reads.
Main-session results carry usable region metadata at 92.7% for reads while
subagent results carry it in 0 of 48,303 events.

## Arms Ladder

Mined conflict sites will be re-enacted with real agents, taking the base from
the merge base, each task's intent from its side's commit, and the answer key
from its side's tests, verified green-red-green per side. Six arms will be
compared: sequential, unmediated concurrent on one shared tree, optimistic
isolation (per-agent worktrees merged at integration with validated retries,
the deployed status quo), file locks from agent declarations, a scheduling
coordinator, and contracts with mechanically derived claims plus harvest
integration. The primary metric is correct completions per agent-minute and
per wall-clock hour, with correctness violations and wasted compute (retried
and discarded agent-minutes) reported beside the rate. Sites are
pre-stratified byte-intersecting, same-file-disjoint, and contradictory-task
from the mining classification, and 19 sites passed the validation and
runner gates (10 Click, 1 Pygments, 2 hugo, 6 commons-lang). Cell sizes are
fixed at 5 draws per site and arm with arm order randomized and interleaved
across sites, subject model identifiers and CLI versions recorded per draw,
and no confirmatory draw runs before the pre-registered hypothesis file and
its dated amendments, committed before the mining results landed for the
divergence and intersection claims.

## Statistical Variance

Merges within a repository are dependent, so intervals use a
repository-cluster bootstrap beside Wilson intervals, and co-change results
use a whole-commit cluster bootstrap because a commit touching k files yields
k(k-1)/2 dependent pairs. Rates are reported with their denominators in the
same sentence, and a rate whose denominator is censored or unavailable is
reported unavailable rather than zero.

## Participants

Analysis workers are Codex agents (gpt-5.6-sol) and the arms ladder will run
Codex agents as subjects first, extending to Gemini and Claude on later
approval. Agent-subject runs will meet a measured clean-room bar (environment
manifest and a same-day calibrated planted-marker canary, certificates beside
the data), following the standard established for LEAN-Bench.

# 4. Results

## Base Rate

416 of 25,073 evaluable merges conflicted (1.66%), spanning 0 of 1,556 (ydb)
to 124 of 219 (hugo, 56.6%) per repository. Workflow dominates language, with
one monorepo recording zero two-parent merges in 1,387 commits under a squash
workflow. Half of all merges carried under one day of divergence, showing the
human base rate is an operating point produced by early integration rather
than a property of software. Conditioning on both sides carrying a nonempty
diff against the base raises the rate to 416 of 17,749 (2.34%), which
narrows but does not close the gap to prior conflict-rate reports in the
high single digits. The strictest comparable conditioning (both sides
touching a common file) is not recoverable from the mined rows, which retain
no per-side path sets, and reconciling the remaining gap against the prior
corpora is named as open rather than resolved.

## Divergence

Conflict probability rises monotonically in all three exposure measures, from
0 of 5,356 at one combined commit to 195 of 2,100 (9.3%) at 128 or more, and
from 51 of 12,156 (0.42%) under one day to 71 of 952 (7.5%) at 30 to 90 days.
Within-repository AUC is 0.815 for commits, 0.746 for time, and 0.801 for
lines, with 12 of 12 informative repositories above 0.5 and none below.
Fitting the hazard in changed lines gives logit(p) = -5.955 + 0.234 log(1+x)
with the slope 95% interval [0.115, 0.353] excluding zero, and pushing
measured agent write sizes through that curve predicts a textual collision
rate of 0.51% for a median task pair and 0.78% at the ninetieth percentile.
The prediction sits below the human base rate of 1.66% because agent writes
are small, with a median of 9 changed lines against branch diffs in the
hundreds, so the fleet-level rate is governed by concurrency rather than by
per-pair risk. The prediction is registered before the arms run.

## Granularity

The median conflicted span in handwritten files is 10.7% of the file's bytes
(Q1 to Q3 of 3.8% to 26.7%) and 23 of 845 measurable handwritten conflicts
(2.7%) are whole-file. Lockfile conflicts have a median span of 100% with 33
of 45 occurrences concentrated in the top 1% of files, vs 437 of 8,157 (5.4%)
for handwritten files, showing coordination hotspots are largely a lockfile
phenomenon. Changed byte ranges genuinely intersect in 304 of 372 decidable
conflicted merges (81.7%) and are same-file disjoint in 46 of 416 (11.1%).
The intersection measurement is not circular with the conflict definition.
The conflict comes from the three-way merge over hunk windows with context
while the byte ranges come from each side's zero-context diff against the
merge base, a different object, and the adjacency band the merge algorithm
flags appears separately as the boundary-only class (22 of 416). The 44
censored merges (10.6%, lacking a full stage triple in 42 of 44 cases and
concentrated 31 of 44 in hugo) bound the rate at 304 of 416 (73.1%) if every
censored merge were disjoint and 348 of 416 (83.7%) if every one intersected,
so the majority-intersection claim holds under worst-case censoring.

## Over-Blocking

Across Click's full anchored census a file claim would have serialized 475 of
684 same-file concurrent path units (69.4%) that merged cleanly, and 249 of
458 units (54.4%) inside globally conflicted merges. A uniform contiguous
placement null over 797 measured span pairs expects 86.8% of same-file pairs
to be disjoint against the measured 69.4%, and within the same cohort real
edits overlap 6.8 times more often than the null predicts, so edits cluster
strongly rather than scattering across the file. The semantic replay found
0 of 44 clean merges with green parents broke the suite, with the caveat that
only 13 of 44 contained same-file concurrency and 4 contained concurrent
source paths. Developers hand-modified the recorded tree in 1 of 398 clean
merges, removing a duplicated changelog entry.

## Causal Dependency

Across validated tasks a change's focal tests broke under perturbation of
9.5% to 29% of swept files, established by intervention. One task did not
reproduce between two runs (9 of 31 vs 10 of 31), so these rates inform
design and are quarantined from customer-facing claims until the
non-reproduction is diagnosed.

## Attribution

9 of 13 contested path observations in the production corpus were fully
attributable (69.2%, and 66.7% to 70.0% under three alternative counting
rules), with one staged change vanishing where the on-scene adjudicator could
not distinguish a deliberate revert from another writer's overwrite. Of 11
collision events the stated deciding basis was a safety boundary in 3,
staleness in 3, compatibility in 3, and a measured red result in 2, and 0
were decided by test presence alone.

## Capture

Subagent file operations carried usable claim metadata in 0 of 48,303 events
and shell commands outnumber structured calls 3.29 to 1 at 35.2% parser
recall, so claims cannot be reconstructed from transcripts even in principle.
The hottest files by reader multiplicity are the coordination documents
themselves (a standing-orders file read by 24 distinct actors), showing the
file-based coordination bus is the system's own contention hotspot.

# 5. Limitations

The mined repositories are a selected corpus rather than a probability sample
and every rate is conditional on two-parent merges surviving in history. The
semantic replay measured one repository in a recent purposive window. The
production and transcript corpora cover one team on one harness with a
Node-dominated workload, and the causal instrument has one undiagnosed
non-reproduction. All conflict rates are human operating points, and the
agent-conditioned rates are the subject of the unrun arms ladder rather than
a claim of this paper.
