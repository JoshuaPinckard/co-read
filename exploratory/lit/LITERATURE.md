### Ghiotto’s conflict anatomy: what it does and does not measure

Ghiotto et al. measure substantially more anatomy than a generic “conflict/no conflict” indicator: conflict-chunk counts per failed merge and per file; the number of lines on each side of each chunk; enclosing Java constructs; dependencies across chunks; and resolution strategies. They report that 40% of failed merges have one chunk and 90% have at most ten, while 62% of conflicted files have one chunk and 95% have at most five. ([paper PDF](https://leomurta.github.io/papers/ghiotto2018.pdf))

For size, Ghiotto et al. report that 94% of 175,805 chunks have at most 50 LOC on each side, 68% at most 10 LOC, and 50% at most 5 LOC; side-specific means are 19.5 and 27.6 LOC and medians 2.0 and 2.5 LOC. These are **line counts**, not bytes, and the paper does not derive independent base-relative byte ranges for the two sides or test those byte ranges for intersection. ([paper PDF](https://leomurta.github.io/papers/ghiotto2018.pdf))

Accordingly, “prior work has not measured conflict anatomy/size” is false. The narrower byte claim in the novelty audit below survives this paper. ([Ghiotto PDF](https://leomurta.github.io/papers/ghiotto2018.pdf))

### Brun’s H2 precedent numbers

For the three Figure 4 systems on which build and test outcomes were available, the published table gives 1,694 completed merges: 266 textual conflicts, 24 build failures after a text-clean merge, 109 test failures after a successful build, and 1,295 clean merges. The unconditional rates are therefore 15.70%, 1.42%, 6.43%, and 76.45%. Cascading the table exactly as the method describes gives 24/1,428 = **1.68% build failure among text-clean merges**, 109/1,404 = **7.76% test failure among successful builds**, and 133/1,428 = **9.31% either build or test failure among text-clean merges**. These percentages are calculations from the published cell counts, not separately printed estimates. ([paper PDF, Figure 4](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~brun/pubs/pubs/Brun11fse.pdf))

The surrounding prose says both “5,355 merges” for these projects and “33% of 399 merges that version control reported as clean.” Those statements cannot be reconciled with Figure 4: the three rows total 1,694, and 399 equals 266+24+109, i.e. all three conflict classes rather than the text-clean denominator. The prose-based denominator is therefore **NOT VERIFIED**; the table-derived stage rates above are the defensible H2 precedent. ([paper PDF](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~brun/pubs/pubs/Brun11fse.pdf))

### Conditioning reconciliation paragraph

The cleanest event-definition comparisons to this project’s **all-two-parent-merges** rate are Leßenich et al. (10.99%), Owhadi-Kareshk et al. (8.12%), and Mahmoudi et al. (8.75%), because all three replay historical, exactly-two-parent Git merge commits for textual conflict; their Java/language constraints, repository selection, replay implementations, and sampling still differ ([Leßenich PDF](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~ckaestne/pdf/jase17.pdf), [Owhadi-Kareshk PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.06274), [Mahmoudi PDF](https://sarahnadi.org/assets/pdf/pubs/MahmoudiSANER19.pdf)). Accioly et al. also use exactly-two-parent historical scenarios but a hybrid semistructured/textual outcome; Cavalcanti et al., Dias et al., and Brun Figure 4 use historical scenarios with other tool/language filters or without a verified exact-parent restriction ([Accioly PDF](https://pauloborba.cin.ufpe.br/publication/2018understanding_semi-structured_merge_conflict_characteristics_in_open-source_java_projects/2018ESESemistructuredMergeConflictCharacteristics.pdf), [Cavalcanti PDF](https://pauloborba.cin.ufpe.br/publication/2017evaluating_and_improving_semistructured_merge/2017OOPSLASemiVsUnstructuredMerge.pdf), [Dias PDF](https://pauloborba.cin.ufpe.br/publication/2020understanding_predictive_factors_for_merge_conflicts/2020ISTPredictiveFactorsForMergeConflicts.pdf), [Brun PDF](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~brun/pubs/pubs/Brun11fse.pdf)). No project conditioning exactly matches Cassandra; if forced, its constructed shadow-master events are conceptually nearer **both-sides nonempty** because they apply a nonempty local changeset into evolving master, but the paper does not establish that the master side differs from the changeset’s base for every event ([Cassandra PDF](https://web.engr.oregonstate.edu/~sarmaa/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/2486788.2486884.pdf)). Zimmermann’s 22.75–46.62% is the nearest comparator to **both-sides common file**, because its denominator excludes file updates that did not require same-file integration, although its unit is a CVS file update rather than a Git merge ([Zimmermann PDF](https://thomas-zimmermann.com/publications/files/zimmermann-msr-2007.pdf)). Ghiotto’s manual sample is conflict-enriched and not explicitly restricted to two parents, its automated anatomy corpus is conflict-conditioned, Apel’s sample is conflict-enriched, and Brun’s Figure 5 constructs speculative opportunities; none supplies a population-aligned base rate ([Ghiotto PDF](https://leomurta.github.io/papers/ghiotto2018.pdf), [Apel PDF](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~ckaestne/pdf/esec11.pdf), [Brun PDF](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~brun/pubs/pubs/Brun11fse.pdf)). Thus denominator conditioning explains some of the high literature rates—especially Zimmermann—but not the gap to the closest exact-parent historical studies; repository selection, language, era, sampling, and merge strategy remain plausible study-design explanations rather than verified causal explanations. No verified study in this table labels its denominator a continuous-integration replay population: Cassandra’s shadow-master events and Brun’s speculative opportunities are constructed integration scenarios, while the other rate-bearing rows use completed historical merge commits or CVS update events ([Cassandra PDF](https://web.engr.oregonstate.edu/~sarmaa/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/2486788.2486884.pdf), [Brun PDF](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~brun/pubs/pubs/Brun11fse.pdf), [Zimmermann PDF](https://thomas-zimmermann.com/publications/files/zimmermann-msr-2007.pdf)).

## 4. Novelty audit

### (a) Byte-sized conflict regions plus intersection of the two sides’ byte ranges

**Verdict: NONE FOUND in the named corpus or the targeted search sweep.** This is a bounded literature-search result, not a proof of global absence. ([Ghiotto PDF](https://leomurta.github.io/papers/ghiotto2018.pdf), [Mahmoudi PDF](https://sarahnadi.org/assets/pdf/pubs/MahmoudiSANER19.pdf), [Campos Junior TOSEM manuscript](https://leomurta.github.io/papers/camposjunior2025.pdf), [Pijul manual](https://pijul.org/manual/theory.html))

The broad anatomy claim does **not** survive. Ghiotto et al. measure chunk counts and per-side LOC distributions ([paper PDF](https://leomurta.github.io/papers/ghiotto2018.pdf)); Accioly et al. classify nine semistructured conflict patterns and normalize by methods, chunks, and lines ([paper PDF](https://pauloborba.cin.ufpe.br/publication/2018understanding_semi-structured_merge_conflict_characteristics_in_open-source_java_projects/2018ESESemistructuredMergeConflictCharacteristics.pdf)); Leßenich et al. count consecutive conflict lines and experiment with conflict size in lines ([paper PDF](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~ckaestne/pdf/jase17.pdf)); and Mahmoudi et al. analyze 258,956 Java conflict regions, reporting line-based mean/median sizes of 39.63/12 with a refactoring and 26.63/7 without one, and intersect line ranges for refactorings/evolutionary changes with conflict regions ([paper PDF](https://sarahnadi.org/assets/pdf/pubs/MahmoudiSANER19.pdf)). None of these computes the intersection of independently derived, base-relative **byte** ranges for the two merge sides.

Two still-closer papers do not close the gap. Campos Junior et al. measure conflict/candidate sizes in LOC in the 2022 study ([paper PDF](https://leomurta.github.io/papers/camposjunior2022.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3555228.3555229)) and in **characters** with character-level LCS similarity in the later TOSEM work ([author manuscript](https://leomurta.github.io/papers/camposjunior2025.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3748256)); those are not byte-range intersections between independently reconstructed side edits. ([Pijul manual](https://pijul.org/manual/theory.html))

**Surviving paper wording:** “Prior work measures merge-conflict anatomy at chunk, LOC, syntax, and character granularity; in the literature reviewed, we found no empirical study that both measures historical conflicted-region sizes in bytes and intersects the two sides’ independently derived base-relative byte ranges.” ([Ghiotto PDF](https://leomurta.github.io/papers/ghiotto2018.pdf), [Accioly PDF](https://pauloborba.cin.ufpe.br/publication/2018understanding_semi-structured_merge_conflict_characteristics_in_open-source_java_projects/2018ESESemistructuredMergeConflictCharacteristics.pdf), [Mahmoudi PDF](https://sarahnadi.org/assets/pdf/pubs/MahmoudiSANER19.pdf), [Campos Junior TOSEM manuscript](https://leomurta.github.io/papers/camposjunior2025.pdf))
